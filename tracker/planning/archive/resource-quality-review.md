# Resource quality review

Reviewed **2026-10-21**.

The original catalog had **497 external entries and 34 authored decks**. This review removes **155 entries from the visible app**, adds **9 targeted worked examples**, and leaves **385 visible resources including decks**. Reading identities and existing saved data remain intact.

Subsequent addition on 2026-10-21: the researched **Data architecture: how we got here** deck and executable lab bring the visible catalog to **386 entries (35 authored decks)**. This addition is recorded separately in the JSON companion; the original audit counts above remain historical.

## Standard and scope

Retain worked use cases, meaningful tool instructions, specific implementation/API references, production lessons and core chapter-mapped books. Remove definition-only stubs, generic homepages/channels/feeds, redundant alternatives, product/role advocacy, abandoned-project datasets and obsolete walkthroughs. No minimum word count decides quality: a compact API example may be useful, while a long promotional article may not be.

Every catalog entry received a scope/relevance decision. Automated public retrieval checked 405 targets; article outlines, depth and example structure informed triage, and replacement sources plus ambiguous selections were inspected online. Books were not reread and videos were not watched end to end. JavaScript shells, temporary 403/429 responses and redirects are recorded as inspection limits, not proof of low quality or a paywall. The JSON companion gives the evidence scope for each entry. The authored decks were checked for registration and curriculum/lab mapping, not rewritten or re-reviewed slide by slide.

The new modeling sources include actual schema/ETL/snapshot SQL and worked relationship cases. They replace the previous short Kimball technique stubs and just-in-time modeling opinion piece. The historical Kimball fact-table design tip is retained because it develops a concrete business process example; retirement is about the page’s utility, not its author.

## Important changes

- Modeling: end-to-end dbt build, physical table design, SCD1/SCD2 ETL, late-member handling, fact loading, snapshot interval joins and many-to-many trade-offs.
- Performance: replace chapter landing pages with actual composite-index and hash-join examples.
- dbt/testing: prefer GitLab’s real dbt procedures and executable test references over generic testing-workflow advice.
- Semantic metrics: replace product positioning and benchmark claims with a Jaffle Shop model/query walkthrough.
- References: repair the moved PyArrow Parquet page while preserving its key.
- Discovery: keep Data Engineering Weekly and Simon Willison; remove unselected podcast feeds, most newsletters and YouTube channel directories.

Raw `L`/`LX` records remain resolvable for old imports, exports and reading ticks.
